## Venmo for Business: How It Works

Venmo is no longer just a peer-to-peer payment app. With Venmo business profiles, sellers can accept payments from Venmo's large user base. Since Venmo is owned by PayPal, it uses similar infrastructure but with a different fee structure.

Any Venmo user can create a business profile for free. Once set up, you get a dedicated business page where customers can pay you directly through the Venmo app. There's no monthly fee and no setup cost.

## Venmo Business Fee Structure

Venmo business transactions are charged a seller fee. The buyer pays nothing extra.

**Standard Venmo business fee:**
- **1.9% + $0.10** per transaction

This applies to payments received through your Venmo business profile. The fee is automatically deducted before the funds hit your Venmo balance.

**Important distinctions:**
- **Personal transactions** (sending money between friends) are free
- **Business profile transactions** are charged the 1.9% + $0.10 fee
- **Venmo credit/debit card payments** at checkout on external sites use PayPal's standard rates, not Venmo's business rate

## Venmo vs Stripe

How does Venmo compare to the other major payment processor?

| Feature | Venmo Business | Stripe (US) |
|---------|---------------|-------------|

| $50 sale fee | $1.05 | $1.75 |
| $100 sale fee | $2.00 | $3.20 |

Venmo's rate is lower than Stripe's, but Stripe offers far more features for online businesses: full website integration, subscription billing, invoicing, and international payment support.

## When Venmo Makes Sense for Sellers

Venmo business profiles work best in specific scenarios:

**Good fit:**
- Local service providers (barbers, tutors, personal trainers)
- Small sellers at markets, pop-ups, or craft fairs
- Freelancers with clients who already use Venmo
- Side hustles where simplicity matters more than features
- Social selling (Instagram, Facebook Marketplace)

**Not a good fit:**
- E-commerce stores needing website checkout integration
- Businesses with international customers
- Subscription-based businesses
- B2B services requiring invoicing
- High-volume sellers needing detailed reporting

## Venmo Business Tax Reporting

Venmo is required to report business transactions to the IRS. Starting from tax year 2024, the threshold for Form 1099-K reporting has been lowered:

- **Current threshold:** $600 in total business payments per year
- If you receive $600 or more through your Venmo business profile, Venmo will send you a 1099-K

This means even small sellers need to track their Venmo business income for tax purposes. Keep records of all business transactions and associated expenses.

Personal Venmo payments (between friends) are not reported unless they're flagged as business transactions.

## Fees on Venmo Instant Transfers

When you want to move money from your Venmo balance to your bank account, you have two options:

- **Standard transfer (1-3 business days):** Free
- **Instant transfer (minutes):** 1.75% of the transfer amount (min $0.25, max $25)

If you need cash quickly, the instant transfer fee adds to your total cost. On a $500 transfer, that's an additional $8.75. For most sellers, waiting 1-3 days for the free transfer is the better choice.

## How to Calculate Your Real Venmo Costs

Your total cost per transaction depends on whether you use instant transfers:

**Without instant transfer:**
- Fee on a $50 sale: $1.05 (2.1% effective rate)

**With instant transfer:**
- Payment fee: $1.05
- Instant transfer fee: $0.88 (1.75% of $50.30)
- Total: $1.93 (3.86% effective rate)

With instant transfers, Venmo becomes more expensive than Stripe. Only use instant transfer when you actually need the money immediately.

## Tips for Venmo Business Sellers

1. **Keep personal and business payments separate.** Use your business profile for all commercial transactions. Receiving business payments through your personal account violates Venmo's terms and won't have proper tax reporting.

2. **Use standard transfers.** The free 1-3 day transfer saves 1.75% compared to instant transfer.

3. **Set clear payment expectations.** Tell customers your Venmo business name so they pay the business profile, not your personal account.

4. **Track everything.** Download monthly statements from Venmo for your records. Unlike PayPal or Stripe, Venmo's reporting tools are minimal.

5. **Consider your growth path.** Venmo works for small-scale selling, but as you grow, you'll likely need a more robust payment processor with invoicing, subscription billing, and website integration.

## The Bottom Line

Venmo's 1.9% + $0.10 rate is one of the lowest available for US sellers. If your customers are already on Venmo and you don't need advanced e-commerce features, it's a cost-effective choice. But for online stores, international sales, or growing businesses, Stripe or PayPal offer more functionality despite higher fees.
